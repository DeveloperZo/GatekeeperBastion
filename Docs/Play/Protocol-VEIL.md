# VEIL Protocol

## Game

This sheet is about who is looking at me, which turns out to be the only question I ever really had.

Its doctrine changes what a decoy is. Placed while I wear this, a decoy comes up already holding the attention I was carrying, instead of pulling neutrally from whoever is near. I hand the room's interest to a machine and walk out of the room.

The crew burst is the loud one. For the window, every Gatekeeper renders through walls, for everyone — the most useful thing anyone can hand a crew — and for the same window, every Default and Hunter in the place is coming for me. The crew gets the map. I get the room. That is not a drawback. It is the price on the tin, and I choose where to set the tin down.

The personal burst takes me off every targeting list outright. Anything mid-swing finishes the swing. Firing puts me back on instantly, so it is a way to leave, never a way to win. The room forgets me exactly as long as I give it nothing to remember.

The passive is standing: they notice me closer in than they otherwise would. Not silence, not stillness — just less reach on being seen. Standing still was never the requirement. It took me three Boardings to stop believing it was.

## Reference

Claimed at CREW QUARTERS, on the same panel as Default.

### Doctrine: PHANTOM

A decoy you place spawns holding the attention you are currently carrying — everything already provoked onto you — rather than pulling neutrally. The payload's own live count is unchanged; this is about what the decoy inherits, not how many stand.

### Crew: Ping

For the window, every Gatekeeper renders through walls, for every Boarder. For the same window, every Default and Hunter targets you.

Thieves are unaffected in both directions, as always: they cannot be pulled and they do not shout.

The output belongs to the crew, the cost belongs to the caster. Solo, you get the render and the pull with nobody to hand the map to, which makes it a deliberate mass-provoke and occasionally exactly what you want.

Values and bindings: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

### Personal: Cloak

Off every targeting list for the window. Gatekeepers already swinging finish the swing. Firing exits it instantly.

### Passive: Ghost

Gatekeepers notice you at a reduced fraction of their normal acquisition range. It applies always, moving or still.

### How it plays

- **Ping is a pull you chose.** Every Default and Hunter in the Bastion arrives on you. Press it standing somewhere you can fight a crowd, never while backed into the Hatch bay, and never at the end of a Wave when you are already hurt.
- **Cloak is for leaving, not for winning.** The instant you fire you are visible again. Use it to break contact, to cross a room, to get back to the Hatch, or to survive a Ping you called a moment too early.
- **Ping then Cloak is the sheet's whole shape in co-op.** Mark the room, take the attention, drop off the list, and let the crew shoot a garrison that is walking the wrong way.
- **Ghost changes where fights start, not whether you win them.** With it you can hold a longer sightline than you could otherwise, which is worth more on this game's open floors than it sounds.
- **None of this helps against a Thief.** It never looked at you and it never will.

## Gaps

- Ping's pull is not distinguished on screen from its render. Players read the through-walls effect as the whole ability and are surprised by the arrival.
- Nothing indicates you are cloaked to the garrison rather than merely unnoticed, and nothing warns that firing ends it.
- Ghost has no readout at all. A permanently reduced acquisition range is invisible and is the sheet's most valuable row.
- That a PHANTOM decoy inherits what is already provoked onto you is never stated. It looks like an ordinary decoy that happened to work well.
- Ping does not exclude Thieves in its description, so players expect it to move them.
