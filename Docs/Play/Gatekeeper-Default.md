# Default Gatekeeper

## Game

The plain frame. Everything else here I describe by how it differs from this.

On a field-terminal Wave it walks at me. On a Hatch-bank it walks to the Hatch and will pass straight by me to get there. It closes and swings. Contact only.

Three things put one on me. Shooting it. Being seen by it at distance across a clear line. And standing in its space, which is the only one of the three that wears off. The first two are permanent for the rest of its life, and when it flips it also picks up pace. Faster than my walk. Slower than my sprint. Backing away while firing is a slow loss, not a stalemate.

Hurt one and it shouts, and everything near it drops what it was doing and joins. The ones it pulls do not shout again. One shot buys one group's attention, which is either a tool or an accident depending on whether I meant it.

Once one reaches the Hatch it stops negotiating. It will keep hitting while I shoot it, while I stand beside it, while its friends chase me. It is a decision only while it is still walking.

Individually the weakest thing here. It wins by count, and the surplus from a raised intensity is entirely this.

## Reference

### What it does

It walks to the Hatch and hits it — on a Hatch-bank. On a Wave started at a field terminal the Hatch is out of the fight, so it walks at you from the start.

It is also the only kind that can change its mind, and it changes its mind in one direction only: **once it decides you are the problem, it hunts you for the rest of its life.** There is no cooling off, no losing it, no breaking line of sight and having it wander back to what it was doing. It also picks up speed when it flips — a provoked Default is faster than your walk, so backing away from one while shooting it does not create distance. Sprinting does.

### What flips it onto you

- **Shooting it.** Any damage from you, at any range, and it is yours permanently.
- **Being seen.** If it has clear line of sight to you at a distance, that alone is enough, and it is just as permanent as being shot. This is the one that catches people out: holding a long sightline while a group walks in means the whole group arrives already hunting you, and you never did anything to cause it.
- **Standing near it.** Unlike the other two, this one is temporary. Get out of its space and it goes back to the Hatch.

### The cost of pulling one

Shooting a Default makes it **shout**, and every Gatekeeper close enough to hear joins the hunt. Those recruits do not shout again, so the damage is contained to the group — but the group is the unit these things arrive in, so in practice one shot at one body buys you the whole group's attention.

This cuts both ways and you should be deliberate about which way you are using it:

- **Pulling on purpose** takes a group off the Hatch and puts it on you, in the open, where you can fight it on ground you chose. That is often the right trade, and it is the only way to move Defaults at all.
- **Pulling by accident** — clipping a Default while shooting past it at a Hunter, or spraying into a crowd — hands you a fight you did not schedule, usually while your attention is elsewhere.

The Thief is the exception in both directions: shooting a Thief alerts nobody, and no shout ever reaches one.

### The one you cannot pull

Once a Default gets to the Hatch, it is finished negotiating. It will keep hitting the Hatch while you shoot it, while you stand next to it, and while its friends chase you around the room. Provoke decides who they chase on the way in; nothing decides anything once they arrive.

**A Default is a decision only while it is still walking.** Every one you let close is a Default that has stopped being a choice and started being a timer on the Hatch.

### Fighting them

They are individually the weakest thing to kill in the game and they win by count. What actually kills you is a Default group that flipped as one and reached you as one — the swing is not large per hit, but several of them in contact at once is a health bar disappearing quickly, and you do not heal during a Run.

Late in a Run they are the same Gatekeeper with substantially more health. Intensity's extra bodies land entirely here, so high intensity is a bet specifically on your ability to chew through more Defaults, not on anything else.

## Gaps

- Nothing distinguishes a provoked Default from an unprovoked one except that it eventually walks at you. There is no alert, no marker, and no audio cue for the flip.
- The game never says that provocation is permanent. Backing off from one looks like it should work and never does.
- The game never says that being *seen* provokes one. Players reasonably assume only damage does.
- Nothing tells you that shooting one alerts its neighbours, or how far that reaches.
- A Default that has reached the Hatch is unrecoverable by any means except killing it, and the game presents that as the AI simply ignoring your fire.
- Provoked movement being faster than your walk but slower than your sprint is never stated, and it is the difference between kiting working and not working.
