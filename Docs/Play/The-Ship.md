# The Ship

Between runs you are stood in your own ship, in first person, with a gun you cannot fire. This is the only place Salvage gets spent, and the only way back to the Bastion.

You walk. You look at a console until the prompt appears at the bottom of the screen, you hold the interact key, and a panel opens: your movement locks, the cursor comes free, and the panel owns the screen until you close it. One panel at a time. Close it and you are walking again.

Every panel, whichever console it belongs to, shows your banked Salvage at the top. A row you cannot afford does not grey out — it tells you exactly how much more you need. That is deliberate: "I clicked BUY and nothing happened" is not a thing that should be possible.

The header in the corner of the screen lists which direction each console is in. If you are lost, read it.

## What each console is for

Each console owns exactly one subject. Nothing is sold in two places.

**ARMORY — the weapon in your hands.** Weapons, and the upgrade wheel for a weapon you already own. This is also the only place you decide which owned weapon fills your Primary slot and which fills your Secondary; in the Bastion the weapon keys only swap between the slots you are already carrying, never between things you own. Buying a weapon equips it into its own slot straight away. Upgrades are bought per weapon, so Salvage poured into one gun stays in that gun — buy a different Primary and its upgrade wheel starts at nothing.

**CREW QUARTERS — you.** How much punishment you take, how long you can sprint, how much each kill pays, and your only source of critical hits. Repairing the Hatch is not here; that is the Repair Rig at FABRICATION. See [Upgrades](Upgrades.md).

**FABRICATION — the Hatch.** How much the door can take before it falls, plus the equipment slot and the Repair Rig, which is what lets you repair the door at all. What it buys applies from your next embark onward, not retroactively.

**NAV MAP — where you go, and going there.** One destination. A selector for which Wave you start on, capped by how deep you have got before. And the EMBARK button, which is the point of no return: once the Bastion loads, the only exits are clearing it and losing.

## The start-Wave selector

The NAV MAP will let you begin partway in. It does not skip anything — starting deeper delivers every Wave up to that point back to back, with no Prep in between, and then the Run continues normally to the end. You get the same Run with the early half compressed into one shapeless fight. It is a way to stop replaying Wave one, not a shortcut, and the overlap is the price.

## Embarking with nothing

The NAV MAP will warn you loudly if you own no weapon and then let you go anyway. You are not blocked. You will be carrying an improvised cutter and you will not enjoy it. The game is willing to let you make a bad bet; that is consistent with everything else in it.

## What to buy first

**The free sidearm at the ARMORY, before anything.** It costs nothing, and the only reason it is sold rather than given is so that the first thing you learn is that weapons come from that console.

**Then CREW QUARTERS.** Weapon upgrades and the equipment slot are locked until you have lost a Run — attributes are not. Before your first loss, attributes and a weapon are the only things your Salvage can become, so there is no argument about it.

**Of the attributes, Scavenging first if you intend to keep playing.** It raises what every Gatekeeper is worth for the rest of the campaign, and it compounds with the siphon dial rather than competing with it. Everything else you want to buy gets cheaper in real terms once it is in.

**Then whatever killed you.** If a Hunter ran you down, that is sprint. If you died standing in the open trading damage, that is health. If the Hatch fell while you were still standing, that is FABRICATION and not you at all.

**Save the big weapon purchase for when you know which fight you want.** A Primary is fixed for the whole Run, and its upgrade wheel does not follow you to the next gun. Buying one is committing to a range and a cadence, not just spending money.

## Gaps

- The ship never tells you that repairing the Hatch costs Salvage. FABRICATION sells the repair rig and quotes its rate, and there is no price per point healed anywhere on the hub. You find out standing at the door mid-Run, with the bill already running.
- Nothing anywhere lays out which purchases are gated behind losing a Run and which are not. There is no one place that shows you the split.
- Nothing warns you before you buy that upgrades are per weapon and never transfer. You can sink a campaign's savings into one gun, buy a different Primary, and discover the investment did not come with you.
- There is no refund, no sell-back, and no respec, and nothing in any panel says so.
- Nothing tells you that opening a console panel locks your movement. You find out by pressing forward and not moving.
- Your save has a Drone slot. No console sells a payload that fills it. It is not a secret to find; it is an empty slot.
- The upgrade tracks all have a ceiling and you only learn where by seeing a row read MAX. Nothing shows you the whole ladder in advance.
- Repairing needs the Repair Rig equipped, and nothing on the Hatch or in the fight says so until you try to repair and nothing happens.
- The start-Wave selector is capped by the deepest Wave you have *cleared*, but the label reads as though it were the deepest you reached. Dying on a Wave does not unlock starting there.
- An older weapon exists in the code and can still sit in an old save. It is not sold, cannot be equipped, and will not reach your hands.
- If a developer command has been used in a play session, nothing saves for the rest of that session. The hub gives you no visible sign — everything looks bought, and none of it is there next time.
