## Game

The far-wall Primary. Full damage across the whole floor, at distances the MR-35 has already given up on. Nothing else I have carried treats the far half of the room as workable.

Slow. Slow enough that a late Wave will drown me if this is all I fire. It is empty by the time I turn around, so a Hunter in my face is the Secondary's job — always, not usually.

It is strongest where I never stand. Leave the Hatch. Fire up the bore when bodies stack in its mouth. One pierced line up a corridor is what the rate is paying for, and the line is worth the wait.

Its ability arms the next shot to pierce further at full damage, and lapses unfired. Arming it and then not firing is the most common way I waste it. The gun forgives distance and punishes indecision.

Its wheel orders clip and reload differently from the MR-35's, so the same focus buys a different gun. Strength is damage. Weakness is rate. It knows what it is.

## Reference

### What it is for

Killing at distances the MR-35 has already given up. Full damage across the whole Bastion
floor. Slow enough that a late Wave will drown you if this is all you fire. Aimed up the
Hatch bore, a pierced line is the payoff for that rate.

**Railshot** arms the next shot to pierce further, at full damage. It lapses if you do
not fire. Bindings and values: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

### How it wants to be used

Leave the Hatch. The gun is strongest where you currently never stand. Fire up the bore
when bodies stack in the mouth. A Hunter in your face is the Secondary's job.

### What it is bad at

**Volume.** A late Wave floods the room faster than you can delete it. **Contact.** This
gun is empty by the time you turn around. Do not expect it alone to carry the last Wave.

### Tiering it

The ring exists. Strength is Damage; weakness is Fire Rate. Clip and Reload sit in a
different order than on the MR-35, so the same focus buys a different gun. Award and
prices: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).
