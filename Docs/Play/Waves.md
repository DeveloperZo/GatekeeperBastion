# Waves

A Run is a fixed number of Waves, each one drawn by you, each one bigger than the last. The counts, the composition table and the exact scaling are in the [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md); this page is what the escalation *does*.

## Four things escalate, and they escalate together

**More bodies.** Every Wave sends more than the one before it, before the dial is applied at all.

**Tougher bodies.** Each Wave multiplies every Gatekeeper's health — all kinds, the same multiplier. The kind that died to a burst in the first Wave takes noticeably more by the last. This is the axis players notice last and it is the one that most often makes a familiar weapon stop working.

**Wider groups.** Gatekeepers arrive in groups through one spawn point at a time. The group gets wider each Wave, so what spawn points together is a bigger clump.

**Shorter gaps.** The pause between groups shrinks each Wave. Combined with the wider groups, this is the real escalation: early on you deal with a group and then wait; late on the next group is already inside before you have finished the last.

None of these four are affected by the siphon dial. The dial adds bodies on top.

## Composition: what shows up, and when

The kinds are not tiers. They arrive on a fixed schedule that the dial cannot touch.

- The **first Wave is all basic Gatekeepers** — the marchers that go for the Hatch and turn on you only if you hurt them. Nothing else is in it. It exists to teach you the door.
- **Hunters** enter after that, and their count grows every Wave. They never touch the Hatch. They are the reason you cannot park in front of the door and hold the trigger.
- **Rams** enter later still, and their count grows fastest at the end. They never touch you and cannot be provoked into it. They walk to the door and take a serious bite out of it.

Within a group the kinds are **shuffled, not blocked** — a group can be all marchers, or a Ram escorted by Hunters. You do not get a clean Hunter phase followed by a clean Ram phase; you get a mix at every door, every time, which is the whole reason there is more than one kind.

## The first Wave versus the last

| | First Wave | Last Wave |
|---|---|---|
| What's in it | marchers only | marchers, Hunters and Rams together |
| Health | baseline | multiplied substantially |
| Group | narrow | the widest the game sends |
| Gap between groups | the longest | the shortest |
| What kills you | nothing, if you pay attention | being made to answer three problems at once |
| What kills the Hatch | a marcher you ignored | a Ram you didn't have time for |

The first Wave asks one question: can you stop a thing walking at a door. The last asks three at once — the marchers pulling on the door, the Hunters coming for you specifically, and the Rams that ignore you entirely — and every second you spend on one is a second the other two are unopposed. That is the escalation. It is not that any single Gatekeeper becomes frightening.

## Where the difficulty actually comes from

Not from the bodies. From **overlap**. A Wave's difficulty is roughly how much of it is in the room at the same time, and that rises from both ends: bigger groups arriving with less time between them, taking longer to kill because they're tougher.

This is also exactly what the NAV MAP's deep start does deliberately: it delivers several Waves back to back with no Prep, so the overlap is between Waves rather than within one. Same content, stacked.

## Gaps

- Nothing in the fight tells you Gatekeeper health scales by Wave. The HUD shows your damage and the Gatekeeper's kind, never its health, so the same weapon quietly taking twice as long reads as the weapon being broken.
- Nothing tells you that group width and the gap between groups change per Wave. These are the two numbers you feel most and the two you are never shown.
- Nothing says that Hunter and Ram counts are fixed per Wave and immune to the dial. Players reasonably assume raising the dial multiplies everything.
- Groups arrive at spawn points in a fixed rotation, one point per group. You can learn the rotation by watching, but nothing marks the points and nothing indicates where the next group is coming from.
- The members of a group spawn in a ring around their spawn point, which is why a wide group appears to arrive slightly spread out rather than in single file.
- Every kind swings on the same clock — kinds differ in what they hit and how hard, never in how often. Nothing communicates this, so Hunters read as faster attackers than they are.
- The composition of the Wave you are about to draw is never previewed by kind. The Prep panel gives you a total body count and nothing else.
