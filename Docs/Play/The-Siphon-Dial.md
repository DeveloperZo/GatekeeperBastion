# The Siphon Dial

The dial is the only difficulty decision in the game, and you make it fresh before every Wave.

## What it changes

**Two things, and they are the same thing.** Raising the dial multiplies what each Gatekeeper pays you, and it multiplies how many Gatekeepers the next Wave sends. Both sides read the same table, so payout and body count cannot come apart.

## What it does not change

This is the part worth knowing, because it is what makes the wager honest:

- Gatekeepers are not tougher. Health is set by which Wave you are on, and the dial never touches it.
- They do not arrive faster or in bigger groups. Group size and the gap between groups belong to the Wave, not the dial.
- Every extra body is a basic Gatekeeper — the Hatch-seeking, provokable kind. The dial cannot add a single extra Hunter or Ram; those counts are fixed per Wave.

So a raised dial is never a different fight. It is the same fight with more of the easiest thing in it, and more money per body.

## When you can set it

Only in Prep. Pulling the draw locks it for the whole Assault, and it unlocks again when the Wave clears. That is deliberate: the dial is a pre-commitment, not something you back out of when the room gets loud.

If you start deep from the NAV MAP, the whole chained delivery is one Assault — so the dial you set before that first draw governs every Wave in the chain.

The Prep panel prices both sides for you before you commit: what a kill pays now, what it would pay one step up, and how many Gatekeepers the next Wave brings at each setting. Read it rather than guessing. Keys and the exact reward curve are in the [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

## How to think about raising it

Ask three questions, in this order.

**1. Can you kill the surplus before it reaches the Hatch?** The extra bodies all march on the Hatch and all can be pulled off it by hurting them. Your ceiling is not how much damage you can take — it is how much you can put out between a group spawning and that group arriving at the door. If you are already finishing Waves with the Hatch chipped, more bodies means more chip.

**2. What is your health going to look like afterwards?** Health does not come back during a Run. A bigger Wave is more swings landed on you, and that damage carries into every Wave after it. The dial's cost is paid across the rest of the Run, not just this Wave.

**3. Is the Hatch already hurt?** Repairing costs banked Salvage per point of health. If you raise the dial and the door takes more damage, some of the extra income goes straight back out through the repair. The dial pays gross; the door charges net.

Some practical shapes:

- **Early Waves are the cheap place to raise it.** They send the fewest bodies and the softest ones, so the same multiplier buys the same percentage on a smaller, more manageable pile.
- **Late Waves are where a raise actually hurts**, because the surplus lands on top of a Wave that already has the most Hunters and Rams in it and the toughest bodies.
- **A dial you can't finish is worth nothing.** Losing the Hatch mid-Wave stops the income immediately; the multiplier only pays on Gatekeepers you actually kill.
- **Turning it back down is free.** Nothing punishes stepping down in Prep, and nothing commits you to a setting across Waves.

## The one thing the dial is not

It is not a difficulty setting for the Run. It is a per-Wave bet, and the game does not remember it. Every Prep is a fresh decision.

## Gaps

- Nothing says the dial is locked during an Assault. The shortcut panel simply stops showing it, which reads as a bug rather than a rule.
- Nothing warns you that starting deep from the NAV MAP means one dial setting governs the entire chained delivery — several Waves' worth of bodies, at a multiplier you chose before seeing any of them.
- The reward multiplier is a hand-authored curve with uneven steps. The panel shows the current and next values but never the shape, so "one more step" is worth a different amount at different points and you cannot see that coming.
- Salvage is floored to a whole number per kill, so part of a raised multiplier is quietly rounded away on every body.
- Nothing tells you that the dial's surplus is entirely basic Gatekeepers. Without that, raising it reasonably reads as "the Wave gets harder in every way", which is the wrong model and makes the dial look worse than it is.
