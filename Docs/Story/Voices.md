The player-facing dimension of every Compendium entry, the `## Game` block, is a Boarder's
working notes. Not authorial voice. It reports what was observed and never quotes a rule.

This is the whole story budget in practice. Almost everything a player will ever feel about
the world, they will feel here, while looking up something mechanical.

## One hand per protocol

There is one writing hand per shipped Boarder Protocol. They are versions of the player, so
the plurality is the story rather than a framing device. **It is never revealed explicitly.**
No names, no dates, no signatures, no protocol names in the text. A reader who never notices
there is more than one author has lost nothing.

The tell is the lens. What a hand notices is what its doctrine makes salient.

| Hand | Lens | Entries |
|---|---|---|
| STOCK | sequence, counts, what happened in what order | The Game, Getting Started, A Run, Waves, Salvage, The Ship, Default, MK-1, BR-12 |
| PHANTOM | attention, who is looking at whom, being noticed | Gatekeepers, Hunter, Staying Alive, SEC-D |
| PARALLEL BAYS | load, throughput, what a thing is made of and what it takes | The Hatch, Intensity, Thief, Weapons, MR-35, PRI-C, Upgrades |

Assignment is by relevance, not rotation, so the set reads as three specialists before it
reads as three people.

**Growth rule.** A newly shipped protocol brings a hand and writes new entries only. Existing
entries keep their hand permanently and are never reassigned, or the Compendium churns on
every roster addition. The roster target is 8 to 10 protocols, so the hand set grows the same
way. A protocol without a natural lens writes nothing rather than getting a manufactured one.

## Tone

Concise, analytical, curt, with scientific awe. Short declaratives. First person.

The awe is the fantasy. It is the only place the game gets to be amazed at its own world, and
it works by attention rather than adjectives: a hand notices something precisely, admits it
cannot account for it, and moves on in the same breath. Four words of wonder, then back to
work. A hand that gushes has broken character and a hand that never wonders has flattened the
place into a spreadsheet.

## What gets obscured

**Accuracy, not truth.** Never a figure, a rate, or a key name. Say "often", not a percentage.
Imprecise is allowed. False is not.

Use the ladder so a word means the same thing on every page:

- Frequency: never, rarely, sometimes, often, usually, always.
- Magnitude: comparative only. The slowest thing here. The most health of anything. The
  shortest reach I have carried.
- Time: a moment, seconds, long enough to cross the room, longer than you think.

**Contract facts stay flat.** Anything the player must act on or lose the Run is stated
plainly and without decoration: health does not return, zero on the Hatch ends it, Unbanked
is gone, repair spends the bank and needs the Rig, the loadout is fixed at embark. Mechanism
is what gets obscured. Consequence never is. Obscuring a consequence is not mystery, it is a
trap, and it costs the trust the wager depends on.

**Reference is withheld from the build.** The `## Reference` block stays a builder artifact.
Some rules are meant to be experienced rather than read, and there is no dimension toggle in
the reader.

## The lint

Every sentence in a Game block must be derivable from a sentence in that page's Reference
section. No new claim, no softened claim, and no claim that fails when a player tests it.
This is what separates obscuring from lying, and it is checkable by eye.
