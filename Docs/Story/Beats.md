Beats 1 through 6 are shippable now and none of them needs new content. Beat 7 has an open
ruling in [Open-Rulings](Open-Rulings.md).

Each beat gives the felt experience first, because that is the part that has to work. The
carrier and the cost are underneath it.

## 1. The work is clean

**In the chair.** You know the job by the end of the first Prep. Cut in, hold the door,
strip the place, go home. Nobody explains anything because nothing needs explaining, and
that competence feels good. You are not a chosen anything. You are somebody who does this.

The first time you dock with a full bank you understand the whole game.

**Carrier.** `../Play/Getting-Started.md`, which is pure sequence and reads as one person
explaining a job to the next person who will do it. The Situation and A Boarding volumes
generally.

**Cost.** Nothing. This is the current state of the docs.

**For.** Establish that returning is not a topic. The early Runs teach that the game is a
loop of embark and dock, and nobody frames that as strange, because to everyone involved it
is not.

## 2. Your kit answers to what they answer to

**In the chair.** You buy a protocol and it changes what your hardware does without changing
what your hardware is. Then you fight a garrison where every frame takes the same health
multiplier, swings on the same clock, has no weak point and resists nothing. Two rosters
running one rule set. It reads as clean design, and it is, and it is also true in the
fiction.

**Carrier.** `../Play/Weapons.md`, `../Play/Upgrades.md`, `../Play/Gatekeepers.md`. A hand
records the symmetry as convenient and does not pursue it. The protocol roster already plans
to quarry new abilities from the Gatekeeper roster itself, so this beat gets more true as
the game grows rather than less.

**Cost.** One or two observation lines in entries being rewritten anyway.

**For.** Plant the shared source as an engineering coincidence. A tidy design note, not a
clue.

## 3. The hand changes

**In the chair.** You are reading the Compendium for a mechanical answer and the writing
feels different from the last page. Not worse. Differently attentive. One entry counts and
sequences. Another is preoccupied with who is looking at you. A third talks about the room
like a load-bearing structure.

Most players register this as personality. Some register it as people.

**Carrier.** The whole Compendium. One hand per shipped protocol, unnamed, undated,
unsigned. See [Voices](Voices.md).

**Cost.** Nothing beyond the voice pass already specified. This beat is that pass.

**For.** The first evidence that there has been more than one of you, delivered with no
framing at all.

## 4. They are returned too

**In the chair.** You have killed everything in this room several times and the room does
not care. They come in groups, from doors you cannot open, at a rate nothing you do changes.
Clearing a Wave clears a Wave. It does not reduce anything.

At some point the question stops being how many are left and becomes where they are coming
from, and the game declines to answer.

**Carrier.** `../Play/Gatekeepers.md` already ends with the hand admitting it does not know
where they come from and that the rooms do not open for it. That line is the beat.

**Cost.** One line, already written.

**For.** Symmetry with your own return, stated only as a thing that cannot be inspected.

## 5. The realm remembers what you do not

**In the chair.** You walk your Ship and the record is bigger than your history with it.
Banked Salvage, bastion progress, deepest Wave, the pot. It belongs to the place. Boarders
come through and the place keeps the total.

**Carrier.** The realms model as built. The safehouse record. The end screen's lifetime
counters, which already tick independently of any single Run.

**Cost.** Nothing new. It is a reading of state the game already keeps.

**For.** The persistent thing is the place and the process. You are the part that gets
replaced.

## 6. Recognition

**In the chair.** No scene, no music sting, no line. At some point the pieces are all on the
table at once: the protocol you wear and the protocol in the title, the hands that are not
one person, a garrison resupplied the way you are resupplied, a record that outlives you,
and a door you have held a hundred times without once asking who put it there.

You are not breaking into the process. You are the part of it that does the breaking in.

**Carrier.** Nowhere specific. That is the design.

**Cost.** Nothing, provided standing rules 1 and 3 hold. This beat is made entirely of the
five before it.

**For.** The player gets there themselves or plays a good wave-defense game and never does.
Both are fine outcomes and the game must be equally good in both.

## 7. Repeat, or break

**In the chair.** Master Control is the ending: five sealed Waves, then one Convert
window with an elite in it, then BASTION STRIPPED. It is the best thing that can happen to a Run and, read against the five
beats before it, it is the process completing exactly as designed. Winning is repeating.

The other exit is unbuilt. The shape that fits what exists: breaking the process costs the
record. Everything the realm remembers is what lets the loop run again, so the record is the
only thing you could spend to stop it. That makes the choice expensive in the currency the
game has spent every Run teaching you to protect, which is the only way a choice like this
carries weight.

**Carrier.** The end screens, if and when the break ending is authored.

**Cost.** Real work, and none of it is on the critical path for the co-op demo.

**For.** A choice between the ending the game has always offered and one that costs
everything the place remembers.
