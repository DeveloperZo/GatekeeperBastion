The story is not the point. The Boarding is the point. This folder exists so a session
feels like it is happening somewhere real, to someone, for a reason, and so the words
in the game pull in one direction. It is not an arc to finish.

The session this dresses is a short chaotic Boarding with friends — closer in function
to League of Legends' ARAM: Mayhem than to a campaign. You bring a kit, you start the
wave, you fight one lane through someone else's ship, you dock or you strip it, you go
again. The fiction never becomes the win condition.

**The lane this folder protects.** The party-night FPS where friends own the silence,
choose the kit, start the wave themselves, and hold one Hatch — sci-fi as wallpaper,
one more Boarding.

It is not Guns n Goblins' bell (solo, incremental, they rebuild a Bastion). It is not
Helldivers' war (drop, extract, satire is the product). It is not Deep Rock's cave (the
swarm interrupts the job). It is not ARAM (random champion, PvP). Fail those four and
the lane is gone.

The order to read it in:

- [Premise](Premise.md). The fantasy, what everything in the world is, the wallpaper
  underneath, and the rules that keep it small. If only one file survives, this is it.
- [Beats](Beats.md). The felt moments of a Boarding, each tied to something in the
  build. Not a plot in order.
- [Voices](Voices.md). Who writes the Compendium. This is the main delivery mechanism
  and the place the fantasy reaches the player as text.
- [Open-Rulings](Open-Rulings.md). What Zo has not decided, and the assumptions made
  in the meantime.

## The budget

Three carriers, and nothing else:

- the Compendium entries in `../Play Mechanics/`, which are being written anyway (the
  in-game reader copies them to `Assets/StreamingAssets/Compendium/`);
- state the game already tracks and already shows;
- at most one repeated line at the Hatch, if a beat cannot land without it.

A beat that needs a fourth thing is a beat that is wrong.

## Do not build

- A narrator, an AI companion, a voice on the Ship that knows things.
- Collectible lore: logs, audio diaries, terminals with backstory on them.
- A named character anywhere in the Compendium.
- A Gatekeeper that talks, or an elite that exists to deliver a line.
- Any entry, prompt or screen that raises how the Boarder returns.
- A revelation moment, an achievement for reaching it, or a check for whether the
  player understood.
- An ending whose job is to complete a thesis (break the process, seal the source,
  spend the realm). Escaped and Docked are session ends. They are not plot.
