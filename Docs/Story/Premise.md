## The fantasy

You are alone in somebody else's ship with a door at your back, and everything in the room
is walking toward one of you.

That is the feeling the fiction has to protect. Three things make it work and the story must
never get in front of any of them.

**The quiet is yours.** Nothing arrives until you call it. No timer, no ambush, no director
deciding you have rested long enough. Standing in an empty room deciding when to be in
danger is the rarest thing this genre offers, and it is the first thing a player notices
about this game.

**The wager is honest.** You raise the stake yourself, before you commit, and the game does
not cheat you for it. More pay and more of the plainest machine. Not a harder fight wearing
a mask. A player who understands the bet can take it with their eyes open, and being trusted
like that is most of the pleasure.

**The room is a place, not a corridor.** Entries rotate, sightlines cost you, pressure moves.
You pick ground. The Bastion has to feel like somewhere with a floor plan somebody else
designed for their own reasons.

The failure state is deliberately soft. What you banked stays banked. The Runs are dangerous
and losing one is not. That is what makes the wager tellable at all, and the fiction should
never make loss feel like grief.

## What the things are

Short definitions so nothing gets invented twice. Nothing here contradicts the language
authority at `../_meta/_definitions/language.md`; this is the fiction under those words.

**The Bastion.** A held vessel, not a wreck. Somebody built it, garrisoned it, and is still
resupplying it. The spawn rooms do not open for you because they are not yours. It should
read as infrastructure with a purpose you were not told.

**The Gatekeepers.** Machines. Four frames, not four paints. They are not defending
themselves and they are not angry. They are executing a standing order about intrusion, and
the order is older than you are.

**The Hatch and the tether.** Your umbilical, cut into their hull. It is the only thing in
the Bastion that is yours, which is exactly why it is the only thing that can be attacked.
The line is your way home and your leash at the same time.

**Salvage.** Whatever a Gatekeeper is made of, taken. It is not money until it travels the
tether. That is why Unbanked dies with you: it never left the building.

**The realm.** The place, and its memory. A realm holds the Ship, the bastion progress, the
pot and the record. Boarders arrive and are replaced. The realm is the continuity.

**The Boarder.** A suited body with a job. Deliberately not characterised. No name, no face,
no history, no lines.

**The source.** The thing your protocol and their frames both draw on. It is never explained
and never named in the game. The evidence is that both halves obey the same rules, which the
build already does: one health multiplier across every frame, one swing clock, no weak
points, no resistances, and a protocol roster whose new abilities are quarried from the
Gatekeeper roster itself.

## The idea underneath

The Boarder is the Gatekeepers' protocol.

You never question how or why you come back. Nobody in the game questions it either. The
things you come back as are versions of you. Your kit and their frames answer to the same
rules because they draw on the same source. You are fighting, without knowing it, to break
the process or to repeat it.

**The title already says it.** *Gatekeeper's Protocol* reads as the garrison's standing order
until you notice that a *Boarder Protocol* is the thing you claim at CREW QUARTERS and wear
into the Bastion. Same word, two owners. It has been on the splash screen since the project
was named. Never point at it.

## The standing rules

**1. Nobody asks the question.** No line of text, no Compendium entry, no prompt ever raises
how the Boarder returns. Not to answer it, not to tease it. The absence is the tell. One
character wondering aloud converts the arc from something the player noticed into something
the game told them.

**2. The evidence is load-bearing systems, not decoration.** Every beat rides on a mechanic
that exists because the game needed it. A beat that needs a prop gets cut.

**3. No revelation scene.** Recognition is something the player has or does not have, and the
game never checks.

**4. Fun outranks the arc.** If a story rule would make a Run less legible, less fair or less
fun, the story rule loses. This has already cost one idea: the hands do not contradict each
other, because a contradiction reads as a documentation bug before it reads as a story.

**5. The Compendium never breaks its own voice to carry story.** The hands write what a
working Boarder would write. If a beat cannot be said in that voice, it is not said. See
[Voices](Voices.md).
