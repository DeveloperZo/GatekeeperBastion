## The fantasy

You are in somebody else's ship with a door at your back, and the fight has a direction.

That is the feeling the fiction has to protect. Three things make it work and the story
must never get in front of any of them.

**The quiet is yours.** Nothing arrives until you call it. No timer, no ambush, no
director deciding you have rested long enough. Standing at the Hatch deciding when to
be in danger is the rarest thing this genre offers, and it is the first thing a player
notices about this game.

**The start is yours.** You press `T`. The kit you brought is the one you fight with.
The Ante is parked (`Ante.Enabled`, 2026-08-30); Waves run at dial 0. When it returns it
is still a bet you place, not a modifier the game rolls. The live stakes in 0.34.2 are
Withdraw, walking off the Hatch while it can still die, and whether you take the Dock
after a zone.

**The fight has a direction.** A wave begins at the Hatch and ends at a terminal
(`../Design/0-Gameplay.md`). You walk their ship. The Hatch can die behind you. The
Bastion has to feel like somewhere with a floor plan somebody else designed for their
own reasons — a lane through a place, not a ring you stand in, and not a void with a
bridge on it.

The failure state is deliberately soft. What you banked stays banked. The Boardings are
dangerous and losing one is not. That is what makes going again tellable at all, and
the fiction should never make loss feel like grief.

## What the things are

Short definitions so nothing gets invented twice. Nothing here contradicts the language
authority at `../_meta/_definitions/language.md`; this is the fiction under those words.

**The Bastion.** A held vessel, not a wreck. Somebody built it, garrisoned it, and is
still resupplying it. The spawn rooms do not open for you because they are not yours.
The playable spine is a lane through it.

**The Gatekeepers.** Machines. Four frames walk today, not four paints. They are not
defending themselves and they are not angry. They are executing a standing order about
intrusion, and the order is older than you are.

**The Hatch and the tether.** Your umbilical, cut into their hull. It is the only thing
in the Bastion that is yours, which is why it can be attacked, and it is where every
wave begins. The line is your way home and your leash at the same time.

**Salvage.** Whatever a Gatekeeper is made of, taken. A kill credits the realm's bank
the moment the body falls. Unbanked is only what you Withdraw at the Hatch — a pile you
chose to carry, wiped on a down or a breach. Respawn spends the bank, not the carry.

**The realm.** The place, and its memory. A realm holds the Ship, the bastion progress,
the pot and the record. Boarders arrive and are replaced. The realm is the continuity.

**The Boarder.** A suited body with a job. Deliberately not characterised. No name, no
face, no history, no lines.

**The source.** The thing your protocol and their frames both draw on. It is never
explained and never named in the game. It is wallpaper. The evidence that both halves
obey the same rules is design cleanliness, not a clue.

## Wallpaper

The Boarder is the Gatekeepers' protocol. The title already says it. A player who
notices has found a reading; a player who never does has lost nothing. This folder
does not exist to deliver that sentence.

You never question how or why you come back. Nobody in the game questions it either.
Respawn is a priced mechanic. The Howling Abyss does the same job with one line — no
one remembers who fought here, or why — and then the match starts.

## The standing rules

**1. Nobody asks the question.** No line of text, no Compendium entry, no prompt ever
raises how the Boarder returns. Not to answer it, not to tease it.

**2. Systems dress the fantasy; they do not prove a thesis.** Every beat rides on a
mechanic that exists because the game needed it. A beat that needs a prop gets cut. A
mechanic that moved (Convert leaving the demo, Ante parking, kills banking on land) is
restamped here. The fiction does not keep a retired loop alive as evidence.

**3. No revelation scene.** Recognition is something the player has or does not have.
The game never checks, never aims at it, and never ends on it.

**4. The session outranks the backdrop.** If a story rule would make a Boarding less
legible, less fair or less fun, the story rule loses. This has already cost one idea:
the hands do not contradict each other, because a contradiction reads as a
documentation bug before it reads as a story.

**5. The Compendium never breaks its own voice to carry story.** The hands write what a
working Boarder would write. If a beat cannot be said in that voice, it is not said.
See [Voices](Voices.md).
