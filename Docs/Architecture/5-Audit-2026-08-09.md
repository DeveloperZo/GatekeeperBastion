Scope: `Assets/Scripts`, 68 files, read via a staged snapshot of the working tree. Sim files
were checked in full; a handful of Sim files added on 2026-08-08 may be missing from the
line counts. Prompted by Zo's reaction to `RegisterPinHitIfNeeded`.

**Headline: the fear was worse than the finding.** The patterns that mark genuinely careless
Unity code are absent. What is wrong is one structural gap and one measured cost, and both
have a specific fix.

## Clean

Checked and not present:

- **No LINQ** in runtime code. Zero `using System.Linq` in `Assets/Scripts`.
- **No empty `Update`/`Start`/`LateUpdate`/`OnGUI` bodies.**
- **No `FindObjectOfType`/`FindObjectsOfType`.** Already on Unity 6's
  `FindFirstObjectByType` and `FindObjectsByType(…, FindObjectsSortMode.None)`.
- **No `Manager`, `Helper` or `Utils` class** anywhere in the tree.
- **No fake-null bypass on `UnityEngine.Object`.** Every `??=` is on `GUIStyle` and every
  `?.` is on `UnityEvent` or a plain C# type — none derive from `UnityEngine.Object`.
- **`Log.Info` is already `[Conditional("GKB_VERBOSE")]`**, so the pattern is understood.
- **`RaycastNonAlloc` with a reused buffer**, sorted by distance over the returned count —
  correct on the two traps that usually bite (`PlayerGun.Firing.cs`).
- **`QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore` passed explicitly**, which is most "my raycast hit the
  invisible volume" bugs.
- **Ownership labels** (`HOST-ONLY:` / `LOCAL-OWNER:` / `REPLICATED-STATE:`) present across
  the MonoBehaviour set.
- **8 `Update()` methods total** — no update manager needed, and `1-Boundaries.md` §5 says so
  in writing to stop one being proposed.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Sim seam is enforced by a shell script, and the fix is free

`Assets/Scripts/GatekeeperBastion.asmdef` covers `Sim/`, `Game/` and `Util/` with
`"noEngineReferences": false`. `using UnityEngine` in `RunSim` compiles cleanly.
`Tools/verify/run.sh` catches it as a side effect of compiling Sim without Unity references —
real, but it only fires when someone runs the harness.

Audited: `Sim/` uses **zero** of `UnityEngine`, `Vector2/3`, `Quaternion`, `Mathf`, `Color`,
`Random.`, `SerializeField`, `Debug.`. The usual cost of `noEngineReferences: true` is not
payable here because there is nothing to pay it with.

**Fix:** a `GatekeeperBastion.Sim` asmdef with `noEngineReferences: true`; `Util/Log.cs` moves
with it or goes behind an interface. Gate: `using UnityEngine;` in any `Sim/` file fails the
Unity compile. Expect compiles to get **slower**, not faster — adopt it for enforcement.
→ `1-Boundaries.md` §1. **Milestone-sized.**

### 2. `Log.Event` / `Log.Metric` ship in release and evaluate their arguments

Neither is `[Conditional]`. 61 of 147 `Log.*` call sites interpolate; under C# 9 each builds
an `object[]`, boxes every value-type hole, formats, prepends the tag, resolves a stack trace
and writes to the platform log — in a shipped build, whether or not anything reads it.

15 of those sites are in `Game/Player`, `Game/Enemies` and `Game/Combat` — per kill, per
group, per reload. A further **47 interpolations sit inside `OnGUI` files**
(`HudController*`, `ShipHud`), which run at least twice per frame. The `OnGUI` ones are the
larger cost and are unaffected by anything done to `Log`.

**Fix:** `[Conditional("GKB_LOG")]` on `Event` and `Metric`, `GKB_LOG` defined in Editor and
dev builds only; `Warn`/`Error` stay unconditional so crash reporting still works;
`Application.SetStackTraceLogType(LogType.Log, StackTraceLogType.None)`. Separately, hoist
per-frame interpolated strings in `OnGUI` behind a "value changed" check.
→ `3-Unity-Runtime.md` §6. **Milestone-sized, and it must not reduce what is logged in the
developer's build — the session log is the project's verification surface.**

### 3. Silent refusals

`PlayerAbility.TryActivate` returns without a word on two of three failure paths —
`if (_cooldown > 0f) return;` and `if (!fired) return;`. Only the no-ability case logs. Zo
pressed `F` on 2026-08-08 and got nothing, and neither the screen nor the log could say which
of three states he was in.

**Fix:** every declined command says why. → `2-Naming.md` §4. Already an entry on
`Brainstorms/Master-Brainstorm.md` marked for a milestone.

### 4. `FireRay` raycasts everything and filters afterwards

`Physics.RaycastNonAlloc(origin, dir, _pierceHits, maxRange, ~0, …)` then walks each hit to a
`Damageable`. With a fixed buffer, geometry and props consume capacity, and a pierce weapon
firing into a crowded line at capacity **silently drops the far bodies** — a gameplay
difference, not a rounding error.

**Fix:** a layer mask for damageable-or-blocking, and check `count == buffer.Length` as a
warning. → `3-Unity-Runtime.md` §5. Small; belongs inside **M31**, which already edits this
method.

### 5. `new WaitForSeconds` inside loops

`WaveSpawner.Groups.cs:77` constructs one per group with a per-wave-constant interval;
`Damageable.cs:135` one per hit flash. Cache a `static readonly` per distinct duration, or one
per wave. Small, real, and the hit-flash one scales with kill count.

### 6. Static `GUIStyle` caches contradict this project's own gotcha

`Architecture.md` gotcha 3 says GUIStyles are "per-instance, never static (static styles
survive domain-reload-disabled sessions holding destroyed font references)". Static cached
styles are present in `ShipHud.cs:177-187`, `StationTerminal.cs:156-170`,
`SplashController.cs:60-62` and `MainMenuController.cs:125-127`.

The gotcha's stated scope is the Bastion HUD and these are Ship and menu scenes, so this may
be deliberate — but the failure mode (domain reload disabled) is scene-independent.
**Either the gotcha's scope gets written down or these are bugs. It should not stay
ambiguous.** → decision needed, not a fix.

### 7. Two files over the stated size cap

`Architecture.md` sets ≤400 lines in `Game/`. `PlayerGun.cs` is 471 and `DevConsole.cs` is
411. Sim's ≤600 holds (`MetaState.cs`, 590 — close). A cap two files violate is not a cap:
either they split or the number moves.

### 8. `TryGetComponent` unused

39 `GetComponent<>` call sites, 0 `TryGetComponent`. Most are `Awake`-cached and correct. The
ones worth converting are the probing calls where the component may be absent — notably
`GetComponentInParent<EnemyGatekeeper>()` on **every damaged body** in `FireRay`, which is
the hottest lookup in the codebase.

### 9. `partial` as the size-management strategy

Five classes across 13 files. Microsoft documents `partial` for generated code, source
generators and concurrent editing — not for splitting a large class. The discriminating
question is in `1-Boundaries.md` §4; applied here, `SceneBuilder` is the weakest case and the
easiest extraction, `GameController` and `HudController` are the strongest.
**`[pending IQ]`** — InfinityQube's position outranks this.

## Not findings

Two things that look like violations and are not, recorded so they are not re-raised:

- **`??=` on `GUIStyle`** — `GUIStyle` is a plain class, not a `UnityEngine.Object`. The
  null-coalescing hazard does not apply.
- **`Camera.main` in `FirstPersonController.Awake` and `ShipHud`** — correctly guarded with
  `!= null`, called once, and no longer the performance problem it was before Unity 2020.2.
