## Game

I cut into a Bastion and strip it. That is the whole job, and nobody makes me do it.

The Gatekeepers were already here. Here before me, here after me, and in between they come up out of rooms that do not open for me — in groups, on a schedule I pay for. Four frames. I sort them the way you sort anything walking at you: by shape, at distance, early.

Behind me is the Hatch, the umbilical to my Ship, the one way home. If it falls, the Boarding is over, and nothing buys a door back. If I fall, the bank can stand me up — the price climbs with every seat bought back — and when the bank is short of the price, the Boarding is over the old way.

Nothing comes until I call it. That took me longest to trust. Prep is mine and untimed. The Ante is parked: Waves run at zero. A Convert is still a bet I can place, once a zone is done and the control gate is still shut.

Salvage is the realm's the moment a body falls. Converted stays with this realm. Unbanked is a pile I pull back out of the bank if I mean to bet. What travels between worlds is what I am: weapons, wheel, protocol, equipment.

Between Boardings I walk my own Ship. Four consoles, four subjects, nothing sold twice.

## Reference

### What a Boarding is

You embark from the Ship (solo hub, or shared Ship when hosting/joining). The NAV MAP states the premise before you leave. You
arrive already tethered. The Bastion is quiet, and it stays quiet until you start a Wave at the
**Hatch**. There is no Wave timer. Prep is yours: repair the Hatch, walk the room,
pick your ground, or stand at the door and think.

Then they come — in **groups**, with a gap between them. Kill them and the Salvage is banked
the moment the body falls. At the Hatch, `T` starts that zone's next Wave; `T` at the far
terminal shuts it off. Hold Interact on the pad beside that terminal to recall to the Hatch.
A Boarding is a fixed number of field Waves. Clear them and the door opens. Walk through
to **Master Control** in the Control Room: kill the oversized Gatekeeper to arm the switch,
throw it, and the Boarding is **Escaped**.
Clearing Wave 5 does not. How many: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md). Bindings: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

### The wager

The Ante is parked. Waves run at intensity 0. The spend path and the curve stay in the
build; nothing a Boarder can press moves the dial. Restoring it: [The Ante](Ante.md).

A Convert is still a bet: Withdraw at the Hatch, then `T` there once the zone is done and
the control gate is still shut. Higher carry, more of the same fight at the door.

### The four things that come at you

They are not tiers of the same Gatekeeper. Each one is defined by something it
**refuses** to do.

A **Default** marches on the Hatch and ignores you — until you hurt it, and then it comes
for you instead. It is the only kind you can pull off the Hatch, and pulling it is a
choice with a cost. It is the plain machine the other three are measured against.

A **Hunter** will never touch the Hatch. It wants you, it is faster than your walk and
slower than your sprint, and it dies quickly. Lean frame — pick it by silhouette. It is the reason you cannot park in front
of the Hatch and hold the trigger.

A **Thief** walks the Hatch on every Wave; shooting it will not shout. Heavy frame. It takes a bite if it arrives — thinner on a field Wave, heavier on a Convert. It is
slow. That slowness is the offer: you have time to deal with it, if you spend that time
on it.

A **Cloaker** advances only while nobody is looking at it. Put it in the centre of your
view and it stops dead and drops its cloak — solid, visible, going nowhere. Look away and
it moves again. Unwatched it wants what a Default wants. Watched it wants nothing, and the
cost is not the holding: it is every direction you are not holding while you do.

The room asks four questions at once, and the answer to each makes the other three harder.

### Losing, and what losing costs

There are exactly two ways a Boarding ends badly: **you die and stay dead, or the Hatch dies.** There is no leave-anytime lever. Death offers a priced respawn paid out of the realm's bank, at a price that climbs per respawn this Boarding; the carried pile is not the purse. Nothing else can pay for it. When the bank is short of the price and no seat still fights, the Boarding ends. The Hatch has no such offer at any price. The whole rule: [Going Down](Going-Down.md).

Survive a Convert, or shut a zone's terminal off, and you get a third path that is neither Lost nor Escaped: hold interact at the Hatch to **dock** — return to the Ship with what you banked. Fail that bank and the prompt never appears. Start the next Wave and it is gone.

Losing is cheap for what already made it through the Hatch. **Converted Salvage stays banked.** Unbanked is wiped if you go down or the Hatch falls. Clearing takes nothing extra — Lost and Escaped differ in what the screen says, which counter ticks, and whether your deepest Wave rises (it rises only on a clear). Docked keeps the bank and does not tick a loss.

The Boardings are dangerous. The failure is not.

### Between Boardings

You walk your own Ship. Four consoles, each owning one subject.

**ARMORY** owns the weapon in your hands — the weapons themselves, and the upgrade wheel
for whichever one you are carrying. Upgrades are per weapon. Your Primary is chosen here
and fixed for the Boarding.

**CREW QUARTERS** owns the protocol you embark wearing: how hardware deploys, a field you
plant for the crew, one hit you chose to eat, and the rule that says how Gatekeepers read
you. You own it from the first session.

**FABRICATION** owns the Ship: how much the Hatch can take, and the Repair Rig — the
equipment without which the Hatch cannot be repaired at all.

**NAV MAP** owns where you go, and is where you commit to going there.

The first weapon is free, and it is still bought at the ARMORY.
