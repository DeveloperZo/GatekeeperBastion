# Getting Started

## Game

First time out, in order. The order matters more than the aim.

Buy the free Secondary. It costs nothing and it is still bought, at the ARMORY, because the first lesson this place teaches is where things come from. Walk the other three consoles so you know what each one owns. Then embark.

I arrive already tethered, near the Hatch, and nothing happens. Nothing keeps happening for as long as I allow it. The first Wave is Defaults only, and called from a field terminal they come for me, not the door. It is the gentlest thing this place will ever send, and it is still a Wave.

Three lessons were billed to my first Runs.

Health does not come back while I stand, and a fresh realm cannot afford to stand me up either — the pot that buys a respawn starts empty. What the first Wave takes off me, the last Wave collects.

The Hatch does not heal itself. It comes back only if I stand at it and hold, only with the Repair Rig, and it bills the bank per point. A fresh realm has banked nothing, so at first the door stays exactly as broken as I let it get.

Weapon Tiers and the equipment slot stay shut until Salvage first converts through the Hatch. Nothing on the panel says so. Now something somewhere does.

Ammunition is not a resource here. I rationed it for two Runs before I noticed nothing ever ran out.

## Reference

You have just installed it. Here is what to do, in order, and the three things that will otherwise eat your first few Runs.

The rest of the **Compendium** starts at [The Game](The-Game.md). Every binding and every number on this page lives in the [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md). Pause lists whatever is live right now and changes as the state changes.

### 1. Get a weapon. This is not optional.

You start on your Ship with nothing. Walk to the **ARMORY** and buy a Secondary — **MK-1** (reach) or **SEC-D** (contact). Both cost nothing, and they are still bought at a console, because the game wants you to learn that weapons come from there and that the Secondary is already a choice.

If you embark without it, you are not blocked — the **NAV MAP** warns you, then lets you go, and you spend the Run with an improvised cutter that does a fraction of a real weapon's damage at a fraction of its rate. It is survivable and it is miserable.

### 2. Walk the rest of the Ship before you leave

There are four consoles and each owns exactly one subject, so you always know where a thing is bought.

- **ARMORY** — the weapon in your hands: the weapons themselves, and the upgrade wheel for whichever one you carry. Upgrades are per weapon, so pouring Salvage into a gun is a commitment to that gun.
- **CREW QUARTERS** — the protocol you embark wearing: how hardware deploys, a field you plant for the crew, one hit you chose to eat, and how Gatekeepers read you. You own it from the first session.
- **FABRICATION** — the Ship: how much the Hatch can take, the Repair Rig, and the Decoy.
- **NAV MAP** — where you go, the Wave you start at, and the button that commits you. On a friends session only the party lead's EMBARK commits the party.

On a fresh save you cannot afford anything anyway. Look at them so you know where they are.
The **NAV MAP** names the Bastion as garrisoned and says the Hatch is your way back to the
Ship — read that line before you press EMBARK.

### 3. Embark, and expect nothing to happen

You will load into the Bastion already tethered, standing near the Hatch. It is quiet, and it will stay quiet indefinitely. There is no timer and nothing spawns until you act.

Spend the first Prep looking around: find the Hatch, find the doors, notice how far the room runs. This is free.

### 4. Walk to a field terminal

The first Wave is all basic Gatekeepers. On a Wave you start at a field terminal they come for you, not the Hatch. No Hunters, no Thieves. It is the gentlest thing the game will ever send you and it exists to teach you the room.

Look at the terminal. Juice if you want. Select — including intensity 0 — then start **one** Wave. On the kit bastion the first terminal takes three of those to open its door; the others take one. After each clear, Prep lasts as long as you want. At the Hatch the same flow: select, then Hatch-bank. You can bank after Wave 1. Bindings: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

### 5. Fight, then choose

Kill the Wave. Prep. Hatch-bank if you want the Salvage on the ship, or walk back to the terminal and pull the next. Survive a Hatch-bank and, until you start the next Wave, hold interact at the Hatch to dock home. The door for that zone opens on a survived bank, not on finishing the terminal. A Run has a limited number of Hatch-banks; when they are gone the Hatch refuses. How many: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

### 6. Strip the field, then Hatch-bank

Clear the field Waves for that door and walk through when it opens. You can Hatch-bank after any Wave, not only then. Unbanked stays until you do. Survive a Hatch-bank and leftover converts. You are still aboard — or you dock. Walk through to **Master Control** in the Control Room. Juice once at the console — it covers the sequence — then select and start. The door seals for five Boarder-hunt Waves, then opens for five Hatch-bank windows. Survive that and the Run is **Escaped**. Go down instead and leftover is gone.

Then spend it. That's the loop.

### The mistakes that cost first Runs

#### Mistake 1: assuming you will heal

You will not. There is no healing in a Run — no regeneration, no pickup, no medkit. Health refills only at a spawn, and a respawn is bought from the realm's banked pot at a price that climbs ([Going Down](Going-Down.md)). On a fresh realm the pot is empty, so your first Runs really are one life each. In co-op, a downed Boarder waits on the Ship with that same priced way back in; the Run holds while any seat still fights or a respawn can be honored.

This changes how you should play from the very first Gatekeeper: taking a swing is not a cost you pay and recover from, it is a permanent bite out of the Run. Avoid damage you could have avoided even when you are winning comfortably.

#### Mistake 2: ignoring the Hatch until it is loud

Both of the two things that end a Run are permanent, and one of them is a door you can repair. Runs are lost at the Hatch because the Gatekeepers get all the attention.

Three things to internalise:

- The Hatch does not repair itself. It only comes back when you stand at it and hold the repair — and only with the **Repair Rig** equipped. Without the Rig, holding repair does nothing at any price.
- Repairing **costs banked Salvage per point of health** — the same purse you buy upgrades with. It is not free, and the game will happily drain your bank to zero doing it. That is usually the right trade, because a lost run stops the income entirely, but decide it rather than discovering it.
- With an empty bank you cannot repair at all. On your very first Run you have nothing banked until a Hatch-bank converts, so the door is genuinely unfixable until then.

Fix it during Prep, when nothing is hitting you. Fixing it during an Assault means standing still at the exact spot everything is walking toward.

#### Mistake 3: expecting to upgrade your weapon before you have banked

Weapon **Tiers at the ARMORY and the equipment slot at FABRICATION are locked until
Salvage first converts through the Hatch.** Docked or Lost after that does not matter —
the conversion is the key. The protocol at CREW QUARTERS and Hatch Armor at FABRICATION
are never locked.

So on a fresh save, banking nothing and going to the ARMORY to make your gun stronger
does not work. Spend early Salvage on Hatch Armor, or hold it. After the first convert,
the weapon tracks open up.

### Also worth knowing on run one

- **Intensity starts at 0. Confirm it.** You can leave it there while you learn. Juice spends Unbanked and only ever adds Defaults.
- **Freeing the cursor stops you firing.** If you free the cursor and then wonder why firing does nothing, click once to recapture it. Binding: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).
- **Ammo is unlimited.** You have no reserve to run out of; reloading always refills the clip. Reload whenever you have a spare second.
