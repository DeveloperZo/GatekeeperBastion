# Waves

## Game

Each Wave is bigger than the last, and it grows in four directions at once. More bodies. Tougher bodies. Wider groups. Shorter gaps. I have counted, and the count only ever moves one way.

Ante adds to the first of those and to nothing else. Checked, rechecked, holds.

The kinds arrive on a schedule I cannot buy, sell, or argue with. First Wave: Defaults only. Hunters after that. Thieves and Cloakers later still, thickest at the end. Inside a group the mix is shuffled, so there is no clean phase of one problem — sorting what is in front of me is the job, every group, every time.

What makes a Wave hard was never any single machine. It is overlap: how much of the Wave stands in the room at once. Early, I finish a group and wait. Late, the next group is inside before the last one is down.

Starting deep from the Ship does the same thing on purpose — the overlap moves between Waves instead of within one. Same content. Less room. No quiet.

## Reference

A Boarding is a fixed number of Waves, started from field terminals or as a Convert, each one bigger than the last. How many Waves a terminal takes is authored per terminal: the early ones on the kit bastion deliver one Wave per start, and the deepest one delivers its whole run back to back off a single start. Convert waits until every field terminal in the zone you are standing in has been finished. Counts and composition: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

### Four things escalate, and they escalate together

**More bodies.** Every Wave sends more than the one before it, before Ante is applied at all.

**Tougher bodies.** Each Wave multiplies every Gatekeeper's health — all kinds, the same multiplier. The kind that died to a burst in the first Wave takes noticeably more by the last. This is the axis noticed last and the one that most often makes a familiar weapon stop working.

**Wider groups.** Gatekeepers arrive in groups through one spawn point at a time. The group gets wider each Wave, so what spawn points together is a bigger clump.

**Shorter gaps.** The pause between groups shrinks each Wave. Combined with the wider groups, this is the real escalation: early on you deal with a group and then wait; late on the next group is already inside before you have finished the last.

None of these four are affected by Ante. Ante adds bodies on top.

### Composition: what shows up, and when

The kinds are not tiers. They arrive on a fixed schedule that Ante cannot touch.

- The **first Wave is all basic Gatekeepers** — the marchers. On a Wave started at a field terminal they hunt you, not the Hatch. On a Convert they march on the Hatch. Nothing else is in it. It exists to teach you the room.
- **Hunters** enter after that, and their count grows every Wave. They never touch the Hatch. They are the reason you cannot stand still and hold the trigger.
- **Thieves** enter later still, and their count grows fastest at the end. On a field-terminal Wave they hunt you, though they can never actually hurt you. On a Convert they go for the door again. Shooting one still does not shout, and it still does not speed up.
- **Cloakers** enter alongside the Thieves and their count grows from there, more slowly than any other kind. They walk at whatever a Default walks at and hit for what a Default hits for — but only while nobody has them in view. Their count is deliberately small: a Wave you could freeze wholesale by looking around would be free Salvage.

Within a group the kinds are **shuffled, not blocked** — a group can be all marchers, or a Thief escorted by Hunters. You do not get a clean Hunter phase followed by a clean Thief phase; you get a mix at every door, every time, which is the whole reason there is more than one kind.

### The first Wave versus the last

| | First Wave | Last Wave |
|---|---|---|
| What's in it | marchers only | marchers, Hunters, Thieves and Cloakers together |
| Health | baseline | multiplied substantially |
| Group | narrow | the widest the game sends |
| Gap between groups | the longest | the shortest |
| What kills you | nothing, if you pay attention | being made to answer four problems at once |
| What the Hatch does | nothing on a field-terminal Wave; the Convert target on a Convert | the same split |

The first Wave asks one question: can you stop a thing walking at you. The last asks four at once — marchers, Hunters, Thieves that will not turn when shot, and Cloakers that move only where nobody is looking — and every second you spend on one is a second the other three are unopposed. That is the escalation. It is not that any single Gatekeeper becomes frightening.

### Where the difficulty actually comes from

Not from the bodies. From **overlap**. A Wave's difficulty is roughly how much of it is in the room at the same time, and that rises from both ends: bigger groups arriving with less time between them, taking longer to kill because they're tougher.

This is also exactly what the NAV MAP's deep start does deliberately: it delivers several Waves back to back with no Prep, so the overlap is between Waves rather than within one. Same content, stacked. That chain still starts from one select-and-start at a field terminal — deep start is the exception that stacks.
