# The Hatch

## Game

One door with a pulse, and everything on this hull is arranged around it.

The Hatch is the umbilical back to my Ship. It has its own health and it is the only thing here being defended. It cannot be moved, closed, or traded away. Zero ends the Run on the spot — the one loss no pot buys back. A dead Boarder can be priced back onto the floor. A dead door cannot.

It starts every Run whole. Whatever I let happen to it last time did not carry.

On a field-terminal Wave nothing can touch it. A Hatch-bank is the window where it can be hurt, and the same window where Unbanked converts — the payout and the damage come through the same slot. Defaults and Thieves bill the door. Hunters never do, at any range, standing on it.

Repair is me at the door, holding — with the Rig equipped, or the hold does nothing at any bank balance. It bills banked Salvage per point healed and will take the bank to exactly zero if I let it.

Survive a Hatch-bank and the door offers something better: hold, and dock home with what I banked. The offer lives until the next Wave starts, and never appears after a failed bank.

The arithmetic worth keeping: a Thief that reaches this door costs more than killing it. Not in danger. In money. That is the whole reason to walk out and meet one.

## Reference

The Hatch is the umbilical junction back to your Ship, at one end of the Bastion. It is the only thing in the Run that is being defended, and it is why you are stood where you are stood.

It has its own health, separate from yours. It cannot be moved, closed, sealed, or traded away. **If it reaches zero the Run ends immediately** — the same Lost ending, counter and screen as a death nothing could pay back. There is no version of the Run that continues without it, and no respawn for a door ([Going Down](Going-Down.md) is a Boarder rule only).

Field-terminal Waves leave it invulnerable. A Hatch-bank Wave is when it can be hurt, and when Unbanked converts to the ship — drip on the Score card, dump when the timer ends. Unbanked hitting 0 does not end the defense. How hard that Wave is, and how much the convert pays, is how many field Waves you carried in before you called it.

It starts every Run whole. Whatever happened to it last time did not carry over.

### What damages it

Only two of the three things that come aboard will touch it.

A **Default** walks at the Hatch and hits it. It will ignore you entirely unless you hurt it, at which point it comes for you instead — which is the trade: you can pull one off the door, and pulling it means it is now your problem.

A **Thief** hunts you on a field-terminal Wave and walks to the Hatch on a Hatch-bank. On that Hatch-bank it will not turn on you, will not defend itself, and will keep walking while you empty a clip into its back. When it arrives it takes a serious bite. It is slow, and that slowness is the offer — you have time to deal with it if you spend the time on it.

A **Hunter** never touches the Hatch. It only wants you.

So the shape of the threat to the door depends entirely on what the Wave brought. A Hatch-bank with Thieves in it is a Wave where the door is the thing at risk. A Wave without them is a Wave where you are.

### Repairing it

Stand at the Hatch and hold interact. It heals continuously while you hold, at a flat rate, but **only if you have the Repair Rig equipped** — without it, holding repair does nothing to a damaged Hatch. Let go and it stops. The rate is in the [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md). Binding: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

Prep select then start while looking at the Hatch, with Unbanked and a slot left, starts a Hatch-bank. That is not repair. A Run has a limited number of Hatch-banks; when they are gone the Hatch refuses. How many: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

Survive that window and the Hatch offers a different hold: **dock — return to the Ship**. It is live until you start the next Wave. Fail the bank and it never appears. Binding: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

If the Hatch is full, Prep, and Unbanked is 0, hold Interact to **withdraw** banked Salvage back into Unbanked. Withdraw does not need the Repair Rig.

**Repair is charged in Salvage, per point healed, out of the same bank you spend at the Ship.** The prompt at the door tells you the rate and totals what the current hold has cost you so far. That prompt is the only place the game ever mentions the price.

You can repair during a Wave, not only in the quiet of Prep. Whether that is a good idea is a separate question — standing still at the door with bodies in the room is exactly where a Hunter wants you.

If your bank is empty, you cannot repair at all. Not slowly, not partially: the prompt changes to tell you there is nothing to spend and the key does nothing.

If your bank is nearly empty, holding repair will heal as much as you can afford and leave you at zero.

### What repairing costs you

More than the number.

**Repair is paid for out of the same Salvage you were saving for a weapon.** Every point you put back into the door is a point that does not go into the thing that would have stopped the door being hit. That is why a Run where the Hatch takes a beating is a Run that costs you twice — you pay for the damage, and then you get back to the Ship and find you cannot afford what you went in for.

The honest consequence: **letting a Thief reach the door is more expensive than killing it, every time.** Not in danger — in money. That is the whole reason to walk out and meet one instead of holding the doorway.

The end screen breaks out what you spent on repairs, if you spent anything. It is worth reading, because it is the only place the Run tells you what the door cost you.
