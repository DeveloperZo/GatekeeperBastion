# MR-35 Rifle

## Game

A Primary for things that will not come to me. One heavy, accurate shot per pull, no spread, and full weight far enough down a corridor that most of a fight happens inside it. Past that the distance starts taking a cut, and the reach runs on well beyond where the BR-12 has given up. Only the PRI-C reaches further, and it buys that with cadence I would not pay.

It pierces. A shot continues through a body into the ones behind it, up to a limit. Walls still stop it; the dead do not soak it. The trick only pays when bodies queue, so I shoot down corridors, never across them. A group abreast is one hit. The same group in file is several, from one pull.

The cadence is slow, and a miss is real time gone. It rewards a steady hand and punishes a spray. If I am holding the trigger, I am holding the wrong gun.

Thieves are what it is for. Their walk is the entire budget I get, and this spends it from across the room without making me stand idle between shots the way the PRI-C does.

The weak spot is a Hunter already inside my range. One is fine. Several, arriving between shots, is how Boardings end. That is the price of taking this over the BR-12, and the price is real.

A Damage focus compounds here better than anywhere else. A Thief dropped one shot sooner is worth more than the step looks on the panel.

## Reference

### What it is

One heavy, accurate shot per pull, with no spread. It carries full damage a long way — far past the distance where the shotgun has stopped mattering — and only then begins to fall off, out to a reach second in the game to the PRI-C's. Where the falloff starts: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md). Its tracer is pale blue rather than amber, which is how you can tell at a glance what is being fired in a co-op fight.

Its distinguishing property is **pierce**: a shot continues through a body and into the ones behind it, up to a limit. Each body takes the hit once, and solid geometry still stops the shot — pierce goes through Gatekeepers, not through walls. Bodies already dead do not soak up the shot's remaining pierce.

Its cadence is slow. The clip is moderate and the reload is not short. You get a steady rhythm of deliberate shots, not a stream.

### How it wants to be used

- **Take the long sightline.** This is the Primary that stays effective at distance without surrendering its cadence — the PRI-C reaches further and fires far less often. Fighting in a doorway wastes what you paid for. Stand where you can see something coming and start hurting it before it arrives.
- **Shoot down the corridor, not across it.** Pierce only pays when bodies are lined up behind one another. A group walking abreast gives you one hit; the same group in single file gives you several from one shot. Reposition to make the line, then fire.
- **Start on Thieves early.** A Thief walks slowly, cannot be provoked, and will not deviate. The time between it entering the room and reaching the Hatch is the entire budget you have, and this weapon can spend it from across the room.
- **Do not waste the rhythm.** The cadence is slow enough that a missed shot is real time lost. Take the extra fraction of a second to be on target.
- **Keep the sidearm ready for anything that gets inside your range.** Something in your face is the case this weapon handles worst, and the swap is instant.

### What it is bad at

- **A Hunter at contact range.** Hunters are fast, come straight at you, and get inside the distance where a slow deliberate shot is comfortable. One is fine. Several arriving at once, while you are between shots or mid-reload, is how a Boarding ends. This is the price of taking the rifle over the shotgun.
- **Crowds that are not lined up.** Pierce does nothing for a scattered group.
- **Missing.** No spread means no forgiveness — a shot that is slightly off is a shot that did nothing at all.
- **Panic.** It rewards a steady hand and punishes a spray. If you find yourself holding the trigger, you are firing the wrong weapon for that moment.

### Which Gatekeeper it answers

- **Thief — yes, this is the answer.** A Thief has by far the most health in the game, hits the Hatch hard on a Convert, ignores provoke, and cannot be pulled off course. Nothing you do influences a Thief except damage, so the only variable is how early you start and how much each shot does. Both are this weapon's strengths. Kill Thieves from range while they walk, and you buy back the seconds you would otherwise spend standing next to one.
- **Default — yes, in numbers.** Defaults are the bulk of every Wave and they march toward the Hatch, which naturally strings them into lines down corridors. That is exactly where pierce turns one trigger pull into several kills. Be deliberate about the shout: hurting one Default pulls its nearby group onto you, and with this weapon you would usually rather they kept walking in a line than turned and spread out.
- **Hunter — the weak spot.** They will reach you. Answer them with distance if you have any, with the Secondary if you do not, and with a different protocol from CREW QUARTERS if it keeps killing you.

### Tiering it

An upgrade purchase moves three spokes at once — the one you centred and its two neighbours on the ring, nothing to the two opposite. It does **not** extend the range or add pierce. Since this weapon's whole case is damage per individual shot, a Damage focus compounds with what it already does better than it does on anything else — dropping a Thief in one fewer shot is worth more than the step size suggests.
