# Going Down

## Game

Going down is a bill, and the realm pays it or the Boarding ends.

The purse is the bank — the same bank the guns come out of. Everything I killed for is already in it, so the way back up is bought with progress I had genuinely made, not with a stake that was never mine. It is the plainest possible statement of what a death costs: this many upgrades, not bought.

The price climbs with every respawn bought this Boarding, whoever it was bought for. The counter is the Boarding's, not mine. One tab, everybody drinking.

Which is the real trade, and it runs the other way from what I expected. I am never broke because I banked; I am broke because I spent. The Tier I bought on the way out, the repair I held too long at the door, the Ante I raised on a Wave I could not finish — every one of them is a death I can no longer afford, and none of them looked like that at the time.

Down, I wait behind the vignette with the offer written on it, priced against the bank. A guest waits on the Ship with the same offer. If the bank is short and nobody is left standing, there is no offer. That is the whole difference between a setback and a loss.

The Hatch is the exception to everything above. When the door falls there is nothing to buy. The Boarding is over, whatever the realm has banked.

## Reference

Every dead seat — guest, host, solo — comes back through one rule: a respawn priced from **the realm's bank**, at a price that climbs per respawn taken this Boarding. The counter belongs to the Boarding and resets with it.

### The price and the purse

Cost is `BaseCost × (respawns taken + 1)` — 150, then 300, then 450 — and it is debited from banked Salvage, the same pile the consoles, the repairs and the Ante all spend from. There is one pot, and a death is simply another thing that costs from it.

There is no charge pool and no second window. A mid-Wave return and a return at a break are priced identically, because the only question the game asks is whether the realm can currently cover the fee.

Nothing else may pay, and nothing else needs to: a seat's own carried pile is untouched by its return, and one seat can never be short while another is flush, because they are drawing on the same bank.

Because kills bank as they land, the affordability of your return rises with every body you put down. That is the point of it: dying is priced against what this Boarding has actually earned.

### The surfaces

The Boarder who owns the screen goes down into a DOWN state: a vignette naming the price and the bank it comes out of, or the reason it refuses. Confirm respawns them at the spawn, healed. A guest waits on the Ship; the wait surface and the NAV MAP carry FOLLOW for a seat that never embarked (free — that seat never died) and a priced RESPAWN for one that did, refused by name when the bank is short of the price.

### What a respawn never does

It never revives the Hatch: the Hatch at zero ends the Boarding whatever the realm has banked. And it never changes the Wave: composition is untouched by a death or a return.

### When there is no offer

A bank holding less than the price cannot buy anyone back. If no other seat is still fighting, the Boarding ends Lost. A crew that has spent the bank down — on Tiers, on repair, on a raised Ante — and then loses its last standing seat is exactly this case: final by arithmetic, not by a special rule.
