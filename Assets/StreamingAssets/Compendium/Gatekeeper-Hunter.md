# Hunter

## Game

The lean one. I get its silhouette, and that is all I get — the Hatch alarm never fires for it, because it has never once touched the Hatch.

It chose me the moment it arrived. Not provoked, not lured, not confused about anything: it walks straight at me, at full pace, for life. It has no second gear because it never needed one.

The Hatch does not exist in whatever it uses for a world. It will not swing at the door while standing on it. So holding the door answers the three kinds that want the door and answers this one not at all — it just delivers it to the worst place in the room to be found.

It is the fastest thing coming and the easiest to kill, and both of those are the same instruction. There is no positioning answer. The answer is the gun, and it gets the gun first.

Open on one walking in with escort and the escort comes too — it arrived hunting but was never provoked, and provoking it shouts. The Thieves beside it do not look up. They never look up.

## Reference

### What it does

It picks the nearest living Boarder the moment it arrives and walks straight at them, at full speed, for life. It has no second gear because it never needs one — it is not waiting to be provoked into caring.

It is fast, faster than you can walk away from, and fragile. It dies to very little compared to anything else on the roster. The answer to a Hunter is always to shoot it; there is no positioning answer.

### Why the Hatch is not a shield

A Hunter is not merely uninterested in the Hatch — the Hatch does not exist in its world. It will not attack it while standing on top of it. It will not be pulled toward it by proximity the way a Default is. There is no distance from the Hatch at which a Hunter stops caring about you.

This is precisely why you cannot park in front of the Hatch and hold the trigger down. Standing at the Hatch is a good answer to Defaults, Thieves and Cloakers and no answer at all to a Hunter — it just brings the Hunter to the place you least want a fight, in the middle of the crowd already there.

### Shooting one has a side effect

Hunters arrive hunting, but they have not been *provoked* — which means shooting one still counts as provoking it, and provoking it makes it **shout**. Every Gatekeeper near it joins the hunt.

In practice: opening on a Hunter that is walking in escorted by Defaults converts those Defaults into a group that is now also coming for you. Sometimes that is what you want, since Defaults on you are Defaults not on the Hatch. Often it is not, because you have added bodies to a fight you were trying to end quickly.

Thieves in the same group ignore the shout entirely and keep walking.

### Fighting them

Deal with them first and deal with them fast. They are the only kind that threatens your health at speed, they are the cheapest kind to kill, and every second one is alive is a second it spends closing on you regardless of what else you are doing.

The Ante never adds Hunters. If a Wave feels like it is drowning you in Hunters specifically, that is the Wave's fixed composition, not your wager — the Ante's extra bodies are all Defaults.
