# Gatekeepers

## Game

Gatekeepers hold the Bastion. You are the Boarder who cut in; they are already home. They arrive from garrison to repel an intrusion — reclaim the room, cut the umbilical, throw you off.

Three kinds, separated only by what each refuses. A Default can be pulled off the Hatch at a cost. A Hunter is already on you and never touches the door. A Thief hunts you on a field-terminal Wave and the Hatch on a Hatch-bank, and shooting it never shouts.

They arrive in groups from rotating entry points. Kill them during a live Wave and Salvage sits Unbanked until a Hatch-bank. Numbers: [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

## Reference

Everything that comes at you on the Bastion is a Gatekeeper. There are three kinds and no others. They all swing on the same clock — none of them attacks faster than another — and they all die the same way, to bullets, with no weak point and no resistances. What separates them is entirely **what each one refuses to do**.

For every number on this page — health, damage, speed, how many of each kind a Wave contains — see the [Field Manual](Field-Manual.md).

### The three kinds


| Kind                             | Wants                                      | Refuses                                |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| [Default](Gatekeeper-Default.md) | you, on a Wave started at a field terminal | nothing                                |
| [Hunter](Gatekeeper-Hunter.md)   | you, from the moment it arrives            | to touch the Hatch, ever, at any range |
| [Thief](Gatekeeper-Thief.md)     | you, on a Wave started at a field terminal | to shout, or to speed up when shot     |


Read that as a set of answers to one question: *can this thing be moved off what it is doing?* A Default can, and moving it costs you. A Hunter is already on you and cannot be moved anywhere else. A Thief hunts you and still will not shout.

That is the whole roster. There is nothing that shoots at range, nothing that heals, nothing that buffs another Gatekeeper, and no elite or boss version of any kind.

### How they arrive

Gatekeepers do not trickle in one at a time. A Wave is delivered in groups, and **every Gatekeeper in a group comes in at the same entry point**, spread around it so they do not land on top of each other. The next group comes in at a different entry point, cycling around the Bastion.

Two consequences you will feel:

- **A group is the unit of pressure.** Whatever a group contains arrives together and stays roughly together on the way in. A Thief never shows up alone if the Wave has anything else left to send.
- **You cannot hold one direction.** The place the last group came from is not where the next one comes from. Picking your ground is about what you can cover and how fast you can turn, not about plugging one entrance.

The mix inside each group is shuffled. The kinds are not delivered in blocks — you will not get all the Hunters first and all the Thieves last. Sorting the group in front of you is the job, on purpose.

### How they path and what they target

Every Gatekeeper walks the floor. Nothing flies, climbs or takes a route you cannot walk yourself, so anything you can see coming is coming the way you would go.

A Gatekeeper that is allowed to want the Hatch walks at it and starts hitting it as soon as it is anywhere along its face — it does not need to find a specific spot. On a Wave started at a field terminal, nobody is allowed to want the Hatch. They walk at you.

Three things put a Default on you (a Hunter is already there; a Thief hunts you without being provoked):

- **You shoot it.** That is permanent. It hunts you for the rest of its life and it speeds up.
- **It sees you.** Clear line of sight at range is enough, and that is also permanent. Long open corridors hand out a pull for free.
- **You stand near it.** This one is temporary — it deals with you while you are close and goes back to the Hatch when you are not.

Being shot also makes a Gatekeeper **shout**, and every Gatekeeper near it drops what it is doing and joins the hunt. The ones pulled in this way do not shout again, so one shot pulls one group rather than relaying across the Bastion. Still: sniping one body out of a group brings the whole group.

**Getting close to the Hatch does not save you on a field-terminal Wave.** Nobody in that fight is walking to the Hatch. They are walking to you.

### How Waves mix the kinds

The first Wave you start is Defaults only. Hunters start appearing after that and Thieves later still, so each new Wave is the previous problem plus one you have not had to solve yet.

Two rules about composition are worth carrying in your head:

- **Hunters and Thieves are fixed per Wave.** Intensity does not add a single one of either. Every extra body intensity buys is a Default.
- **Later Waves make everything tougher at once.** One health multiplier is applied to all three kinds, so a late Thief is not just a Thief that arrived later — it is meaningfully more shooting, and so is everything escorting it.

Later Waves also send wider groups with less time between them. The Wave gets harder in three directions — more bodies, tougher bodies, denser delivery — and only the first of those is something you chose.

If you start a Run at a deeper Wave from the Ship, your first Wave after selecting at a terminal delivers every Wave up to that one back to back, with no Prep in between. The overlap is intentional: you get the later Wave's composition on top of the earlier Wave's leftovers.

### What killing them is for

A Gatekeeper is worth Salvage only while a Wave is live, and that Salvage is Unbanked until a Hatch-bank. Nothing pays during Prep, and nothing pays for surviving.

Being Lost ends the Run, and so does losing the Hatch. Both keep everything already converted. Unbanked is gone.
