# Cloaker

## Game

The teal seams. That is all I get while it walks: a faint outline and its glow lines, somewhere it thinks I am not looking.

It moves where I am not. Put it in the middle of my view and it stops dead and turns solid — a machine standing in the open, caught. It does not fight the gaze. It just waits, and the moment my eyes are somewhere else it is walking again.

So looking at it works, and that is the trap. While I hold one still, I am not covering the door, the corridor behind me, or the Thief on its way home. The Cloaker never costs me health. It costs me everywhere I am not looking, which in a room this size is most of the room.

Two of us can pin two of them. That still leaves the rest of the arc to whoever is not staring at a frozen machine.

Shooting one blind works if the shot connects — it flashes solid for a moment, wherever it is, and a hit is the only thing that makes it show itself without my eyes on it. It takes more killing than the red ones. Not much more.

When nothing has eyes on it, it behaves like any of the red ones: the Hatch when the Hatch is in the fight, me when I get close or make it personal.

## Reference

### What it does

A Cloaker advances only while no Boarder is looking at it. Centre one in your view — at anything short of long range, with nothing solid in between — and it stops moving and drops its cloak: fully visible, fully solid, holding position. Look away and it resumes, cloaked, as if you had never been there.

Watched, it does nothing at all: it does not walk, does not swing, does not go after the Hatch or after you. Unobserved, it is a Default in everything but colour — Hatch on a Hatch-bank Wave, you when provoked or near, same swing clock as everything else.

The freeze is shared. One Boarder's gaze holds a Cloaker for the whole crew, so a second player does not double what the garrison loses to it — two people can hold two Cloakers, and the room is still bigger than that.

### What it costs you

Attention. Holding one still means the arc you were covering is uncovered. Letting it walk means something tough is getting where it wants to be without being watched. Neither answer is free, and neither answer is wrong — that choice is the enemy.

### Finding one you cannot see

While cloaked it is a shimmer with lit seams, hardest to find exactly when nobody is looking for it. Any hit that connects forces it solid for a moment, gaze or no gaze — a blind shot into a doorway is a real question, and the flash is the answer. Killing one drops the cloak for good.

It shrugs off a little more fire than a Default before going down. It hits no harder.
