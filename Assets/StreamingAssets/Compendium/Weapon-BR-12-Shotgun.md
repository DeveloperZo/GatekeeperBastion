# BR-12 Shotgun

## Game

A Primary for things that come to me. One pull throws a cone of pellets, and each pellet is nothing on its own. The shot is strong only when most of them land — which means close, and filling the crosshair.

Damage bleeds off with distance, and at the edge of its reach it does nothing at all. That reach is the shortest of the three Primaries by a wide margin — only the SEC-D in my other hand works at less — and there is no cue for where the falloff starts. A shot that looked like a hit can be a blank.

Pellets stop on the first thing they touch. The cone does not sweep a group — pellets that land on a second body are pellets that failed to kill the first. Pick one body. Give it everything.

Slow cadence, small clip, the longest reload of the three Primaries. Very few pulls between long, vulnerable pauses. Break contact and reload before empty, or swap, which is faster.

Let them close. That is the whole weapon. Against Hunters it costs nothing — they do the closing, and it deletes them.

Wrong tool for Thieves and for long corridors. Start on a Thief early, and count the time as spent, because the Hunters will spend it on me.

## Reference

### What it is

One trigger pull throws a handful of pellets in a cone. Each pellet is weak on its own; the shot is strong only when most of them land, which means it is strong only when the target is close and roughly filling your crosshair. Damage bleeds off with distance — past a certain point each pellet starts doing less, and by the edge of its reach it does nothing at all. Its reach is the shortest of any Primary by a wide margin — the SEC-D is the only weapon in the game that works at less.

Pellets do not pierce. Every pellet stops on the first thing it touches, so the shot is spent entirely on the nearest body in the cone.

Its cadence is slow, its clip is small, and its reload is the longest of the three. In practice that means a very small number of trigger pulls between long, vulnerable pauses.

### How it wants to be used

- **Let them close.** This is the whole weapon. Firing at a Gatekeeper halfway down a corridor wastes most of the shot; waiting until it is on you does not. Against Hunters, which sprint straight at you, this costs you nothing — they do the closing.
- **Count your shots.** You get very few before you are dry, and the refill is long enough that a Gatekeeper can cross a room during it. Break contact and reload *before* the clip is empty, not when it clicks.
- **Or do not reload at all — swap.** When you are dry with something already on you, switching to the MK-1 is faster than reloading. Reload the shotgun when the room is quiet.
- **Fight in doorways and corners, not open floor.** You want a short sightline where things arrive at knife range one or two at a time, and a corner to step behind while you refill.
- **Do not spread your aim.** The cone does not "sweep" a group — pellets that land on a second Gatekeeper are pellets that did not kill the first one. Pick a body and give it the whole shot.

### What it is bad at

- **Range.** Not merely worse at distance: at distance it is close to useless. There is no visible cue for where falloff starts, so a shot that looks like a hit can do almost nothing.
- **Thieves.** A Thief is slow, tough, and walking to the Hatch on a Convert. To hurt it you must be standing next to it — safe on a field Wave where it hunts you without shouting, but with this weapon's clip and reload, killing one takes several full cycles. If the Thief is close to the Hatch when you start, you will not finish in time. This is the shotgun's structural weakness and it gets worse every Wave.
- **Lines of Gatekeepers.** No pierce, so a queue in a corridor is one shot per body.
- **Long defensive holds.** Its reload keeps taking you out of the fight at exactly the moments a crowd is building.
- **Missing.** With this few trigger pulls per clip, a wasted shot is a large fraction of your ammunition.

### Which Gatekeeper it answers

- **Hunter — yes, emphatically.** Hunters ignore the Hatch, sprint straight at you, arrive at close range by design, and have the least health of anything in the Bastion. That is the exact target this weapon was built to delete, and it usually does so in one pull. Wave for Wave, Hunters are the thing that punishes a Primary that cannot handle a target in your face, and the BR-12 does not have that problem.
- **Default — yes, at your ground.** One at a time, up close, in a doorway. Be aware that hurting one shouts and pulls its neighbours onto you: with this weapon that is sometimes what you *want*, because it brings them into your range instead of leaving them at the Hatch. Just do not pull more than a clip's worth.
- **Thief — no.** Nothing about this weapon suits a Thief except that on a field-terminal Wave it will not shout while you stand next to it. If your Primary is the BR-12, plan to start on a Thief early, and expect to spend a long time on it while Hunters use that time on you.

### Tiering it

Damage is raised **per pellet**, and an upgrade purchase moves three spokes at once — the one you centred and its two neighbours on the ring, nothing to the two opposite — so where you put your upgrade focus decides whether the BR-12 gets a harder shot, more shots, or a shorter wait between clips. Range, spread, pellet count and falloff never move.

One quiet consequence: critical hits are rolled per pellet, so this weapon would get more crit rolls per trigger pull than a single-shot weapon. Crit chance is frozen at nothing; there is no Luck track to buy.
